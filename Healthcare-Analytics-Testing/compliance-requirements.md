# Healthcare Analytics Platform - Compliance Requirements

**Status:** Draft for security, privacy, legal, and data-governance review
**Assessment date:** 2026-11-17
**Purpose:** Identify likely legal, regulatory, contractual, and security considerations to be resolved before production processing. This document is not legal advice and does not establish compliance or certification.

## 1. Applicability and Decision Rules

The applicable obligations depend on operating countries, data-subject locations, data flows and hosting, the platform's purpose, and whether each organization acts as a controller, processor, covered entity, business associate, Data Fiduciary, Data Processor, or ABDM ecosystem participant. An analytics platform may receive data under partner agreements and still have independent duties. Map each processing activity and partner before deciding applicability.

| Framework | When to assess applicability | Legal / governance character |
|---|---|---|
| HIPAA and HITECH (United States) | The relevant entity is a HIPAA covered entity or business associate and the information is protected health information (PHI), including ePHI | Statutes and regulations, including Privacy, Security, and Breach Notification Rules; business associate terms may add binding contractual requirements |
| GDPR (EU) / UK GDPR | Territorial criteria are met, including establishment-related processing or, for non-EU/UK entities, applicable targeting/monitoring conditions | Directly applicable regulation (GDPR) or domestic UK legislation/regime (UK GDPR and related law), with national and sectoral supplements |
| Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (India) and rules/notifications | The processing falls within the Act's territorial/material scope and relevant provisions have commenced | Indian statute and delegated rules/notifications; commencement and rules may be phased, so confirm the operative text and dates for the deployment |
| ABDM Health Data Management Policy and ecosystem specifications | An entity integrates with or participates in ABDM building blocks or is bound by associated terms, agreements, onboarding, or program requirements | Policy/program/contract and technical participation requirements; not a substitute for applicable privacy statutes |
| Other local laws and agreements | Sources, subjects, hosting, support, or transfers touch another jurisdiction or sector | Local statutes, professional secrecy, health-record rules, data-localization/transfer requirements, and partner contracts may apply |

**Required legal gate:** Legal/privacy specialists must document jurisdiction, role, purpose, legal basis, data categories, recipients/processors, transfer mechanism, retention rules, and notification duties for every production data flow. Check current consolidated laws, commencement notifications, rules, regulator guidance, and ABDM specifications at design and before each material release.

## 2. Legal Obligations vs Recommended Security Practices

A law may require an outcome while leaving the implementation choice risk-based. The practices in the right column are recommendations unless made binding by the applicable law, regulator direction, contract, ABDM participation requirement, or approved organizational policy.

| Topic | Conditional legal or program obligation | Recommended control and test evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Patient data privacy and purpose | HIPAA limits uses/disclosures of PHI and requires applicable safeguards; GDPR requires a lawful basis, purpose limitation, minimization, transparency and accountability; India's DPDP Act governs digital personal data within scope and provides notice, purpose, rights and security duties; ABDM participants must follow applicable program policy/specifications and agreements. | Maintain data inventory, data-flow map, purpose register, notices, role/recipient mapping, privacy assessment, and requirement-to-test traceability. Verify reports and exports contain only approved purposes and fields. |
| Consent and lawful basis | Consent is not a universal substitute for other legal bases. GDPR requires an Article 6 basis and, for health data, an applicable Article 9 condition; where consent is relied upon, it must meet the applicable conditions and be withdrawable. HIPAA distinguishes permitted uses/disclosures from cases requiring an authorization; partner agreements and other laws may require additional permission. DPDP processing must rely on a permitted basis and meet applicable notice/consent or other provisions. ABDM health-information exchange may require consent artifacts for participating flows. | Product and legal owners define which purposes require consent/authorization and which use another basis. Where consent is used, capture subject/representative, purpose, data categories, recipient, scope, version, timestamp, expiry/validity, and withdrawal/status as required. Test grant, deny, expiry, withdrawal, revocation propagation, stale/replayed requests, and deny-by-default behavior. Keep the consent/authorization record separate from the health payload and auditable. |
| Encryption in transit and at rest | HIPAA Security Rule requires transmission security and risk-appropriate safeguards; its encryption specifications are addressable, not a universal algorithm mandate. GDPR Article 32 identifies encryption and pseudonymisation as appropriate measures depending on risk. DPDP requires reasonable security safeguards as prescribed. Specific contracts, rules, or policy may prescribe more. | Use modern TLS for service and partner links, strong encryption at rest for databases, object storage, backups, and removable media, managed keys, rotation, separation of duties, and secrets management. Test certificate/transport policy, encrypted storage/backup configuration, key access and rotation, and ensure secrets/PHI do not leak to logs. Do not describe a particular cipher or key length as legally mandated absent a governing standard. |
| Access control and identity | HIPAA requires technical access controls, person/entity authentication, and role-appropriate access; the Privacy Rule's minimum-necessary requirements apply where relevant. GDPR requires appropriate security and accountability; controller/processor obligations restrict authorized processing. DPDP requires reasonable safeguards and accountability by the Data Fiduciary. | Use unique identities, least privilege, role- and purpose-based authorization, MFA for privileged/remote access, provisioning/deprovisioning, periodic access reviews, session controls, service-account governance, and tenant/source isolation. Test the complete role-permission matrix, direct API access, exports, privilege escalation, disabled accounts, and negative cases. |
| Audit trails | HIPAA requires audit controls for systems containing ePHI. GDPR accountability, records and security duties require demonstrable controls, though it does not prescribe one universal application audit format. DPDP Rules and applicable sector/program requirements may require security safeguards and records; confirm operative details. ABDM terms/specifications may require traceability. | Record access to sensitive records, exports, privileged actions, consent decisions, configuration and rule changes, and ingestion/replay events. Protect logs from tampering, restrict access, synchronize clocks, alert on suspicious access, and define retention. Test completeness, attribution, time ordering, immutability/access controls, and absence of PHI in routine logs. |
| Retention and deletion | GDPR storage limitation and erasure rights apply subject to exceptions and legal retention duties. HIPAA does not establish one universal retention period for all medical records; state law, other rules, and contracts may set periods. HIPAA-required Security Rule documentation is generally retained for six years under 45 CFR 164.316(b)(2)(i), which is not the same as a universal PHI retention rule. DPDP provides erasure when the purpose is no longer served or retention is not necessary, subject to legal retention and other conditions. ABDM policy/agreements may specify lifecycle rules. | Create an approved retention schedule by data class, purpose, jurisdiction, and copy (raw, curated, cache, logs, backups, exports, test evidence). Test expiry, deletion/erasure propagation, legal hold, backup expiry, and auditable disposition. Avoid keeping data "just in case." |
| Masking, de-identification, anonymization | GDPR pseudonymized data remains personal data if re-attribution is possible; truly anonymous information is outside GDPR only when individuals are no longer identifiable. HIPAA de-identification must use a permitted method (Safe Harbor or Expert Determination) to qualify as de-identified under HIPAA; limited data sets remain PHI. DPDP applicability turns on whether data is personal data under the Act; do not assume masking alone removes that status. ABDM identifiers and policy controls must be handled under program rules. | Prefer synthetic test data. If using de-identified data, document method, threat/re-identification assessment, approvals, key separation, access, and residual risk. Test linkage, rare-value and quasi-identifier risks, small-cell suppression, and separation of re-identification keys. Call data anonymized only after an approved assessment establishes non-identifiability under applicable law. |
| Breach and security incident management | HIPAA/HITECH Breach Notification Rule sets notifications for breaches of unsecured PHI, with timing and recipients depending on facts and thresholds; business associates notify covered entities under applicable terms/rules. GDPR requires controller supervisory-authority notification within 72 hours where feasible unless the breach is unlikely to risk individuals, processor notice without undue delay, and data-subject communication for likely high risk, subject to exceptions. DPDP breach notifications follow the Act and operative Rules/notifications; confirm current format, recipient and timing. ABDM contracts/policies may impose additional escalation. | Maintain an incident plan with 24/7 contacts, containment, evidence preservation, privacy/security/legal triage, risk assessment, notification decision log, partner coordination, recovery, and post-incident review. Exercise tabletop and technical scenarios for exfiltration, misdirected exports, compromised credentials, lost devices, unauthorized access, and test-artifact exposure. Never hard-code one deadline across all regimes. |
| Data subject / patient rights | GDPR provides access, correction, erasure, restriction, portability, objection, and other rights subject to conditions and exceptions. DPDP includes rights and grievance mechanisms under its applicable provisions. HIPAA grants individual rights regarding PHI to covered entities under specified conditions; platform duties may flow through a covered-entity relationship and agreement. | Provide a routable workflow to identify the responsible controller/fiduciary/covered entity, verify requests, search source and derived stores, preserve legal holds, propagate corrections/deletion where required, and report completion within applicable deadlines. Test requests across raw, curated, caches, exports, and partner copies. |
| Cross-border transfers and vendors | GDPR Chapter V restricts international transfers and requires an applicable transfer mechanism. Other jurisdictions may impose localization or transfer requirements. HIPAA business associate terms and security obligations apply to covered vendors. DPDP/sector and partner restrictions must be checked. | Inventory hosting, backups, telemetry, support access, subprocessors, and CI artifacts by country. Assess transfer mechanisms and vendor terms before data flows; test region restrictions and third-party access controls. |

## 3. Control Requirements for the Platform

### 3.1 Consent, authorization, and data sharing

- Do not infer consent solely from an account, source connection, or prior sharing event. Identify the applicable basis and purpose per flow with legal/privacy owners.
- Enforce purpose limitation and recipient/source scope at API, pipeline, query, dashboard, and export boundaries.
- If consent or authorization is required, validate its status and scope at the time of access/disclosure. Record, expire, revoke, and propagate changes reliably; retries and replay must not bypass a withdrawal or revocation.
- For ABDM-connected flows, implement the then-current participant registration, sandbox certification, consent-manager/gateway, consent artifact, and health-information exchange requirements applicable to the participant role. Confirm exact specifications with NHA/ABDM before implementation.

### 3.2 Access, audit, encryption, and resilience

- Deny access by default; separate platform administration, integration operations, analytics, read-only, and support duties. Require elevated approval for break-glass access and audit it.
- Encrypt data in transit and at rest using organization-approved settings. Manage keys separately from data, limit key administrators, rotate/revoke keys, and test restore and key availability.
- Make audit trails attributable, time-synchronized, access-controlled, tamper-evident, and retained per approved schedule. Avoid logging full payloads, access tokens, secrets, or direct identifiers.
- Back up and restore within approved recovery objectives; test replay and reconciliation to ensure restoration does not duplicate or silently drop records.

### 3.3 Test and analytics data

- Use synthetic data by default. Production data must not be copied to CI, ordinary test, demos, screenshots, issue trackers, or analyst sandboxes.
- Treat pseudonymized, masked, and coded records as personal/sensitive data unless the applicable legal test establishes otherwise. Control re-identification keys separately.
- Test fixtures, query output, logs, browser traces, screenshots, backups, and test results are all within data governance. Set access, expiry, secure deletion, and incident handling for them.
- Dashboards and reports must disclose freshness, source coverage, limitations, and approved metric definitions without exposing data to unauthorized roles or implying a clinical conclusion beyond intended use.

## 4. Compliance Verification Checklist

Before release, the accountable owners should confirm and retain evidence for:

- Jurisdiction and role matrix, approved purposes/legal bases, data inventory/flow, data-sharing and processor/business-associate agreements, and transfer assessment.
- Required notices, consent/authorization rules, user rights and grievance processes, retention/deletion schedule, and legal holds.
- Privacy impact assessment/DPIA where required; security risk assessment; approved technical and organizational measures; and supplier/subprocessor review.
- Tested encryption, identity lifecycle, access matrix, audit trail, incident response, backup/recovery, deletion, anonymization/de-identification, and secure test-data controls.
- Applicable incident notification decision tree, contacts, forms, timelines, and evidence preservation process.
- ABDM participation role, current policy/version, onboarding/testing status, and applicable consent/data exchange specifications, if integrated.
- Named legal, privacy, security, data-governance, and service owners accepting their respective residual risks. QA evidence does not itself constitute legal compliance approval.
